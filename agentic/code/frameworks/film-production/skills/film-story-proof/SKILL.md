# Film Story Proof

## Inputs

Read the current production state, brief, available source material, intended tone, and any locked lines or selected performances. Load `aiwg show template film-production-brief` for the story record.

## Workflow

1. Express the story as a concrete situation, character desire, obstacle, action, and observable consequence. For comedy, identify the setup and the change that makes the payoff work; for other forms, identify the intended audience response.
2. Map each beat to what the audience sees or hears, what each character knows at that moment, and why the next action follows. Separate source identity from a character's knowledge of its origin.
3. Build the cheapest useful proof: a written beat sequence, rough storyboard, or timed assembly of existing references and authorized audio. Do not let an available attractive asset dictate an unrelated story beat.
4. Mark each utterance as internal thought, visible speech, off-screen speech, narration, or nonverbal performance. Preserve approved wording and selected takes; show an explicit dialogue diff when revisions change them.
5. Play or read the sequence at its intended pace. Test viewpoint, revelation order, motivated transitions, and setup/payoff. Bound any research to the unanswered story decision and stop once there is sufficient evidence.
6. Record what works, remaining uncertainty, and the smallest useful revision. Continue within established creative authority; seek a decision only when an unresolved creative choice materially changes the requested film.

## Outputs

Update the brief with the beat proof, dialogue versions, timing assumptions, and review evidence. Point current state to this version.

## Continue or hold

Continue to shot planning when the causal sequence and performance intent are coherent. Hold polished dependent production for unresolved premise, viewpoint, or dialogue conflicts. Story acceptance does not establish visual-quality or motion acceptance.
