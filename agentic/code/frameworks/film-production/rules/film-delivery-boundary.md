# Film Delivery Boundary

## Scope

Applies to final conform, exports, review packages, handoffs, recipient delivery, and public release.

## Requirements

- Resolve delivery specifications and destination from the brief and current authority. Distinguish a review candidate, requested draft, accepted master, and published release.
- Keep the project/timeline and required exact-source layers, selected takes, titles, and processing editable. Include dependencies and source/version mappings needed by the agreed handoff; do not flatten essential controls without an explicit delivery reason.
- Verify the actual loaded project/timeline and export range. Check output identity, format, duration, frame rate, audio, readability, and complete intended ending. Play the exported file; successful rendering and metadata alone do not establish audiovisual quality.
- Record delivered versions/hashes, locations, export settings, checks, and known limitations. Verify relinking or reopening when supported; label untested portability rather than claiming it.
- Keep one current delivery/review package. Preserve historical versions without presenting conflicting files as equally controlling.
- Carry existing delivery authorization forward. Local editing/export, external upload, recipient delivery, public posting, and scheduling remain distinct actions; acceptance of a film or draft never implies all of them.
- Do not report recipient receipt or publication from a mere local export, upload attempt, or ambiguous response. Reconcile external operation IDs before retrying a possibly completed delivery.

## Required response

Finish all independent authorized packaging before raising a genuine destination or authority boundary. Provide accessible files, clear status, and material residuals. A requested draft may be delivered with explicit limitations; it must not be relabeled as a verified final master or published work.
