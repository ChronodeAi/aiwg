# Field Reads — Full Frameworks

Source: Shreyas Doshi, "Navigating Your Organization Like a Pro" corpus (NotebookLM `e3dfb770-2b94-4aa7-881c-472393656117`).

## 1. The Babies-in-Disguise Maturity Read

**Baby in disguise** — an adult who fundamentally operates "as just a victim of whatever circumstances there are":
- "Small things irritate them to a fairly large degree."
- "Unable to regulate their own emotions."
- "Excessively reliant on other people to soothe them."
- "Seek constant attention"; "find many many reasons to get upset and cry and get offended."
- "Repeat these baby patterns over and over again... without learning much and without growing up."
- Cannot resolve conflicts alone — require "grown-ups" (managers) to step in over metaphorical "stolen cookies" and "broken Legos."

**Well-formed adult** (borrowed from Netflix's hiring philosophy): independent, emotionally regulated, navigates challenges without constant intervention, does not generate drama.

**How to use the read:**
- "Discard judgment, discard superiority and just objectively hold this idea of baby and well-formed adult."
- Without rare, skilled intervention "this is their destiny" — do not plan to fix them in a meeting.
- Use it to know "what teams to avoid or what companies you want to avoid," who "should no longer be on the team," and as "an input for you to be able to better influence them."
- Self-check: if you feel proud of being the adult in the room, the pride is clouding the read.

## 2. The 3 PM Cultures

Plot two axes: PM **competence** vs PM **influence** over major product decisions.

| Culture | Spot it | Navigate it |
|---|---|---|
| **PM Dominated** | A bottom-quartile PM "is still able to wield a lot of influence mainly because of his or her title." PM as "dictator"; eng/design treated "as resources." Engineers demand everything spelled out in the PRD; eng leaders say "if it doesn't work in the market don't blame us because we built what the PMs asked us to build." | "Nudge it over time to PM guided." Deliberately introduce "tension between PM and engineering on what to build"; invite pushback. Concrete move: invite the EM/tech lead to partner on — or lead — writing the PRD to build mutual empathy. |
| **PM Serviced** | A highly competent PM has "a low degree of influence or at best a medium degree of influence." "Some other function usually engineering or sales is Top Dog"; PM relegated to "PM as housekeeping." | Recognize it as "the root cause of the vast majority" of cross-functional dysfunction. Set expectations accordingly; influence runs through the top-dog function. |
| **PM Guided** | "High competence PM has a high degree of influence... but a low competence PM does not get the same luxury." PM as "tour guide": opinionated on the destination, leaves room to explore. Influence vacuums fill organically with competence from other functions. | "Try to sustain a PM guided culture as long as possible." Accept that "not everybody is going to be happy all the time." If another function fills a weak PM's vacuum, that is the system working, not a bug. |

## 3. Good vs Bad Friction

**Bad friction:**
- Total *lack* of friction: "everybody staying in their lanes and... just pointing fingers elsewhere."
- Title arguments: "well this is what design is supposed to do... what PM is supposed to do."
- Optimizing "what will make my life easier or what will make my function's life easier" over "what will it take to win."
- "Every single engineer on the team needs to be happy otherwise PM is not doing their job" (a happiness-driven eng subculture tell; also watch for promotion-driven eng and virtue-driven design subcultures).

**Good friction:** genuine tension between PM and engineering *on what to build*; pushback on substance.

**Response:** "It's your job to call it out — it's your job to call out the elephant in the room." Shed "the fantasy of being happy all the time."

## 4. Incompetent / Optics-Obsessed Executive

**Spot it:** you are subjected to a "shit planning process" or "horrible planning template" that adds zero value; the exec manages upward perception, not substance.

**Read consequence:** "You cannot solve it" in this meeting. Containment is a separate workflow — see `manage-the-upward-channel` (inside/outside world buffer). In the room itself: give them "the basics of what they need," keep your team out of the blast radius, and do not attempt public correction (see `pick-your-battles` before any move against an exec).

## 5. Pre-meeting purpose discipline (lowercase-p)

Five minutes before any cross-functional meeting:
- **Capital-P Purpose:** the business goal.
- **lowercase-p purpose:** how you want the person to *feel* about you and the team.
- In-meeting behaviors: tailor background anecdotes to show past successful work with their function; ask open-minded questions about how their specific role works at this company.
