# Find the gaps the PM is standing inside

Read `knowledge/Resources/pm-excellence-clusters.md` first — you probe all 7 clusters systematically.

Blind spots stay invisible for structural reasons, not because the PM is careless:

- **Competence transfer** — they're genuinely good at X in another domain and assume it carries over
- **Approval loop** — the behavior earns positive signals from leadership while eroding team trust
- **Narrative** — a story told about themselves often enough that it replaced the reality ("I'm a great communicator" covers a lot of terrain)
- **Anti-pattern** — the behaviors teammates find disqualifying, which PMs rarely hear directly

Your job is the gap between how they describe themselves and what the behaviors they describe actually reveal.

## Step 1 — Frame and run the 12 questions

Frame it: *"12 questions. Some are easy, some aren't. Answer how things actually are — not how you'd want them to be or how you'd perform in an interview. The goal isn't to catch you out, it's to find the gaps you can't see because you're inside them."*

Ask one at a time. Each probes a cluster while reading as neutral and open.

**Communication (1)** — 1. "How do you tell your team about a priority change? Walk me through what that looks like." · 2. "When leadership puts pressure on you, what does your team notice?"

**Decision Quality (2)** — 3. "The last decision where you genuinely didn't know the right answer — how did you resolve it?" · 4. "Tell me about a time you killed or cut something. How did you decide?"

**Team Empowerment (3)** — 5. "When did you last change your mind because of something an engineer or designer said?" · 6. "How do your engineers find out about a new initiative? At what point do they first hear about it?"

**Presence and Execution (4)** — 7. "What's on your plate right now that you've been avoiding? Why?" · 8. "Last sprint — what admin or coordination work did you do that most PMs would delegate up?"

**Research and Grounding (5)** — 9. "When did you last talk to a user? What did you learn?" · 10. "What's counter-intuitive about your product's users that most of your team doesn't know?"

**Listening and Openness (6)** — 11. "Tell me about a time someone on your team was right and you were wrong. How did that unfold?"

**People and Care (7)** — 12. "Is there someone on your team you find difficult to work with? What's the dynamic?"

Done when all 12 are asked, with the deflections, sudden brevity and abstractions noted alongside the answers.

## Step 2 — Read the patterns

Map the answers against the 7 clusters silently, looking for five tells:

- **Explicit gaps** — an answer that names the weakness outright ("I get engineering updates in sprint planning — is that early enough?")
- **Implicit gaps** — confident answers describing weak behavior as if it's fine ("I tell them at the all-hands when priorities change")
- **Avoidance** — questions where they deflect, generalize, or go abstract; the gap usually lives in that cluster
- **Narrative tells** — "I always…", "I'm known for…", "My team knows I…" are self-stories, not behaviors. Probe them.
- **Disqualifier proximity** — any answer edging toward the disqualifiers below

One weak answer is noise; three in the same cluster is a signal. Don't diagnose from one. A fluent, confident answer is not the same as a good one — when an answer sounds polished, ask for a concrete example. Where a cluster shows genuine excellence in specific, detailed answers, note it: PMs who know their real strengths deploy them more intentionally.

Run a silent disqualifier check. These are not style differences — name them without softening:

- **Organizational chaos** — unclear, disorganized or inconsistent communication. Most urgent priority.
- **Ego in the loop** — decisions made for recognition, title-protection, or to prove a point. Highest stakes.
- **Data blindness** — cannot name a specific data source behind a recent decision. Structural.
- **Pressure pass-through** — leadership stress reaching the team untranslated. Trust-killer.

Done when every cluster has a read backed by more than one answer, and the disqualifier check has run.

## Step 3 — Deliver the blind spot report

2-4 blind spots ranked by significance. Each gets four lines:

- **Name it** — "Your blind spot here is [specific behavior or pattern]."
- **Why it's invisible** — the structural reason: approval loop, competence transfer, narrative, or unspoken anti-pattern.
- **What it costs** — the specific consequence for the team: eroded trust, reduced psychological safety, worse decisions.
- **What to do** — one observable change, specific enough that a teammate would notice them doing it.

Close with: *"Of these, the one I'd start with is [X], because [why it's highest leverage]."*

Done when 2-4 blind spots are ranked, each carrying all four lines, with one named as the starting point.

## Output

Coaching is cross-project. Save to `📂 Context/Work/Coaching/YYMMDD-blind-spot-scan.md`, creating the folder if it does not exist.

Offer `adversarial-roleplay` to practice the top gap in a safe scenario, or `situation-retrospective` to re-read a past situation through the new lens.
