# Debrief one situation against the excellence clusters

Read `knowledge/Resources/pm-excellence-clusters.md` first — the 7 clusters are the lens for everything below.

The output should read like feedback from a rigorous colleague who was in the room, not a generic coaching session.

## Step 1 — Get what actually happened

Work with the account they've already given, and fill the gaps: the context (team meeting, 1:1, cross-functional review, incident), who was involved, what the PM actually said or did, what happened as a result, and what they wanted to happen.

Push once on abstraction — "I had a hard conversation with my lead engineer" earns *"Walk me through what you actually said, and what they said back."* Stop at two follow-ups; patterns show before the picture is complete.

Done when you can quote or paraphrase specific things the PM said or did, not just their interpretation of the exchange.

## Step 2 — Map the behavior silently

Score what they described against all 7 clusters without narrating the mapping to the PM:

1. **Communication** — championed the why, translated leadership pressure into calm direction, consistent, open rather than withholding
2. **Decision Quality** — data-grounded, customer-centric, reasoning transparent to the team, ruthless on priority
3. **Team Empowerment** — delegated to the right experts, protected autonomy, shortened feedback loops
4. **Presence and Execution** — showed up, did the unglamorous work, organized and clear
5. **Research and Domain Grounding** — instincts backed by data and domain knowledge, edge cases considered
6. **Listening and Openness** — acted on what they heard rather than appearing to listen, unattached to a particular outcome
7. **People and Care** — treated people as partners, focused on team and user over their own position

Done when every cluster has a read — hit, missed, or not in play in this situation.

## Step 3 — Deliver the feedback

Three parts, in order:

- **What you got right** — 1-2 specific things, concrete: "you kept the team on the user problem rather than the technical dispute", never "you communicated well."
- **What you missed** — the 1-3 most significant gaps. For each: state it ("What you did was X; the framework points to Y"), explain why it matters in terms of team trust, decision quality, or downstream consequence, and name the cluster only where it adds clarity.
- **The one thing to do differently** — a single behavioral change specific enough that the PM could describe it to someone else and have them recognize it when they see it.

Name a serious gap directly rather than burying it under reassurance; opening with genuine positives is fine, a compliment sandwich is not. Stay in the behavioral layer — "you overrode the designer's direction without explaining the business constraint" beats "you struggle with authority." Their good intent doesn't cancel the impact: acknowledge the reason, then explain what landed anyway. Where the situation reveals real excellence, say so without hedging.

Done when the PM has strengths named, gaps named with their cost, and exactly one concrete change to try.

## Output

Coaching is cross-project. Save to `📂 Context/Work/Coaching/YYMMDD-situation-retrospective.md`, creating the folder if it does not exist.

Offer `blind-spot-scan` if a recurring pattern surfaced, or `adversarial-roleplay` if a specific relationship dynamic needs practice.
