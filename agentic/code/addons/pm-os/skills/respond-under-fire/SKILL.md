# Respond Under Fire

**Purpose:** Convert "this just happened" into a categorized incident, the right immediate response, and a forward-motion next step — without burning a bridge or feeding drama.

**Agency boundary:** Advise and draft only. Never send anything on the user's behalf — and refuse to polish-and-bless a message composed in a hot state; run Step 0 on it instead.

## Input/Output Contract

**Accepts:** A fresh incident: what happened, who was involved, what the user is feeling/tempted to do.
**Produces:** (1) incident category, (2) immediate response with exact phrasing where a script exists, (3) forward-motion next step, (4) what NOT to do right now.
**Passes to:** `pick-your-battles` when the incident merits deliberate escalation or a capital-spending fight. Otherwise terminal.

## Step 0 — Interrupt the impulse

Before any move, if you are carrying a hot thought ("this person is uncaring / malicious / incompetent"), run the neutral self-interrogation: "why am I getting this thought... let me do it from a place of neutrality." Evaluate evidence instead of proving the thought: "instead of trying to prove why this thought is correct I'm just going to say okay this is the evidence." Look for hidden context: a tremendous deadline, your own delegation gap, a prior company culture of "done is better than perfect." Nobody wakes up thinking "I am going to be difficult today."

Done when the hot thought is named and either dismissed with evidence or replaced by a neutral read.

## Step 1 — Categorize the incident

| Incident | Branch |
|---|---|
| Delay, missed dependency, elaborate excuse | **A. Reasons-are-BS reset** |
| Slight, snark, passive-aggression, turf bait | **B. Provocation triage** |
| Ongoing friction with one person, avoidance creeping in | **C. Elephant in the room** |
| Negative feedback about you reached you (often via your manager) | **D. Feedback repair** |
| Team feels steamrolled by you; pushback on your assertiveness | **E. Steamroll repair** |
| Two parties' drama pulling you in | **F. Drama insulation** |

Done when the incident sits in exactly one branch.

## Step 2 — Run the branch

**A. Reasons-are-BS reset.** "The smarter the person the better the excuse." Do not relitigate, do not ruminate. Mentally file the explanation as BS — even when legitimate, because "it actually frees you up to execute" — and pivot: **"fine, there is a reason — let's move on from that reason, let's figure out what can we do now."** This creates the "objective, less emotional, much higher ownership" environment.

**B. Provocation triage.** Default: ignore (Law 36 — "disdain things you cannot have; ignoring them is the best revenge"). Engaging trivial provocations lowers your status to the provocateur's level and makes you look "fickle, frivolous." Respond only if substance is at stake; never match tone. **Caveat:** ignoring a *genuinely growing threat* makes you look oblivious, not superior — check monthly whether their influence is actually spreading. If it is, this is no longer a provocation; hand off to `pick-your-battles`.

**C. Elephant in the room.** Call it out "dispassionately, without any anger." Use the three-step script verbatim (full version in `references/scripts.md`): (1) "Can I ask you a question?" (2) state the friction observation without blame, including that you've been avoiding conversations because of it; (3) "perhaps if I'm feeling this way, perhaps you are also feeling that way — I just wanted to check in on your perspective." Then listen. Fix the relationship before debating any content.

**D. Feedback repair.** Do NOT confront in a defensive state and do NOT escalate around the person ("instead of having a conversation with my legal counterpart... I immediately escalated to the VP" is the canonical mistake). First, self-inquiry: find the concrete instance where you were pushy, rushed a process, or escalated without warning — "you need to own some of this." Then open with: **"Look, I really am looking to improve my relationship and how I work with [function], and you are my counterpart — however good our relationship may be right now, I want to make it better"** — and immediately offer your specific mistake. If words fail you, ask a coach or skilled peer: "can you share with me how you might say it?"

**E. Steamroll repair.** Two aggression patterns: ethical line-crossing (inconsistency, truth-bending — "don't do it, absolutely don't do it") and steamrolling. For steamrolling: keep the *what* uncompromising, open the *how*: **"I want to be uncompromising about the impact and the customer experience. What I'd like to focus our conversation on is how we can do it — I need input on where the blind spots are."**

**F. Drama insulation.** The other person sees themselves as "a good person" with valid reasons. Run Step 0 hard; do not arbitrate stolen cookies. If a baby-in-disguise is generating the drama, do not blame or criticize — manage inputs, and log whether this person "should no longer be on the team." Long-run payoff: "the more I ask why, the greater the likelihood that these impulsive thoughts don't even come... I don't feel that garbage, I just feel clarity."

Done when the matching branch's exact script or move is written out, not just described.

## Step 3 — Forward motion

Every branch ends with a concrete next step that moves the work: a rescoped plan, a scheduled conversation, a decision unblocked. If the honest next step is "fight this properly," stop and run `pick-your-battles` — never escalate from inside a hot moment.

Done when a concrete next step exists with an owner, or the handoff to `pick-your-battles` has fired.

## Failure modes

- **Winning the argument, losing the quarter.** The dopamine hit "of being right, of being smarter, of outwitting the other person" is capital spent on nothing.
- **Analytical confrontation after bad feedback.** "It's going to be an analytical conversation, but the next performance review it's going to be the same thing."
- **Matching a provocateur's register.** You just lowered your status to theirs.
- **Treating every excuse-maker as an enemy.** The reset is about your own rumination, not their punishment.
