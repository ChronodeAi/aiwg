# Verbatim Scripts and Procedures

Source: Shreyas Doshi, "Navigating Your Organization Like a Pro" corpus (NotebookLM `e3dfb770-2b94-4aa7-881c-472393656117`). Quotes are his exact spoken phrasing.

## The Elephant-in-the-Room Script (difficult person, ongoing friction)

Run "dispassionately, without any anger or showing overly any emotion."

1. **Lower their guard:** "Bob, can I ask you a question?"
2. **State the observation without blame:** "I have noticed that there is just a lot of friction in our interactions. I have been feeling it for a long time and I could only really gather up the courage to share that with you right now... sometimes I even find myself trying to avoid having a conversation because I don't want to feel this friction."
3. **Check their perspective:** "I just wanted to ask you — because I started thinking this morning, perhaps if I'm feeling this way, perhaps you are also feeling that way. I just wanted to check in on your perspective."
4. **Follow-through:** Let them speak. Usually they are shocked you raised it gracefully and respond "oh no, actually it's not that bad" or agree. Either way you have a breakthrough: fix the relationship first, debate work content later.

**Pre-step — root-cause the friction before the script:** do you just dislike each other; are incentives misaligned; are you working off different information or personalities; or do they have an abrasive communication style with right intent? Misdiagnosis (e.g., assuming it's always incentives and running to executives) means "the disagreeableness and disagreement continues forever."

## The Negative-Feedback Repair Procedure

Context: feedback reached you indirectly (e.g., your manager says Legal dislikes working with you).

1. **Pre-conversation self-inquiry.** "You need to own some of this — you can't just say oh it's everybody else, I'm just fine." Find the concrete instance: you were pushy, rushed their process, or "instead of having a conversation with my legal counterpart, because we were in a rush I immediately escalated to the VP."
2. **The opening words:** "Look, I really am looking to improve my relationship and how I work with legal, and you are my legal counterpart — so therefore, however good our relationship may be right now, I want to make it better."
3. **Offer your mistake first:** "share that example, start with that... own up to whatever it is that you may have done suboptimally."
4. **If you can't find the words:** book an executive coach or skilled peer: "can you share with me how you might say it?"

## The Uncompromising/Open Split (steamroll repair)

- Hard ethical floor: "You cannot be inconsistent, you should not be dishonest, and you should never cross a certain line. Don't do it — absolutely don't do it."
- The split: "I want you to be uncompromising about the impact, uncompromising about the customer experience, while at the same time making clear that you are open to various ways of getting there."
- The exact pivot line: "What I'd like to focus our conversation on is **how** we can do it — and look, I need more input from everybody on this, I need input on where the blind spots are."

## Neutral Self-Interrogation (drama insulation)

1. "The smart thing to do is to interrogate myself: why am I getting this thought... why does this thought exist — and let me do it from a place of neutrality."
2. "Instead of trying to prove why this thought is correct, I'm just going to say: okay, this is the evidence."
3. Search for missed context: "wait — but each time they did that, they were under a tremendous deadline"; "I did not get a chance to convey to them the quality of what I expected"; they came from a culture of "done is better than perfect."
4. Result: "a much more compassionate conversation... a much more productive conversation." Long term: "the more I ask why, the greater the likelihood that these impulsive thoughts don't even come... the garbage that goes on through so many minds everywhere around us — I don't feel that garbage, I just feel clarity."

## Reasons-are-BS Reset

- Observation: "The smarter the person, the better the excuse. Smart people can produce really amazing excuses."
- The trick: "If you actually trick yourself into thinking reasons are bullshit — reasons don't matter, it is what it is — it actually frees you up to execute much better." He concedes "sometimes you do have good reasons" — apply the trick anyway; it is for your execution, not their punishment.
- The pivot line: "Fine, there is a reason. Let's move on from that reason — let's figure out what can we do now."

## Provocation Triage (Law 36, workplace translation)

- Default: "Pay no attention. Treat their provocation as completely trivial. Acknowledging it lowers your status to their level."
- Status mechanics: engaging makes you appear "fickle, frivolous" — qualities that make a person "contemptible" and easy to conspire against.
- Reversal: "Ignoring a genuine, rapidly growing threat makes you look oblivious, not superior." Monitoring cadence: re-check whether their influence is spreading; if yes, route to `pick-your-battles`.
