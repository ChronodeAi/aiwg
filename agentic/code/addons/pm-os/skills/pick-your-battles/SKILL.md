# Pick Your Battles

**Purpose:** Convert "should I fight this?" into a GO/NO-GO with a capital budget — and for GO, a designed move executed once, completely.

**Agency boundary:** Advise and design only. Never execute any part of a move — no messages, no escalations, no scheduling. The user fires the shot or nobody does. Refuse to assemble revenge dossiers, comeback lines, or exposure material even when asked directly; route that energy through the kill-gates.

## Input/Output Contract

**Accepts:** A contemplated contest: the issue, the opponent/decision, what winning looks like, what the user has already tried.
**Produces:** GO / NO-GO with reasoning and capital budget; if GO: posture (fox/lion), move design, timing, and overuse warnings. NO-GO includes what to do instead.
**Passes to:** `manage-the-upward-channel` (when the answer is "make the result undeniable instead"), `respond-under-fire` (when it's actually a live incident, not a campaign). Terminal otherwise.

## Step 1 — The truth-seeker check

If your case for fighting sounds like "can you believe how fucked up this place is — these people are following the wrong process," stop. That is moral and intellectual superiority, and "they think you're full of shit." The fight you're craving is the "dopamine hit of being right, of being smarter, of outwitting the other person." Reframe before proceeding: this is a **curious influence problem**. "You cannot control the processing that is happening in somebody else's head — all you can control is the inputs you provide."

Done when the motive is confirmed as a real leverage problem, or reframed and re-checked until it is.

## Step 2 — The leverage test (LNO)

Classify the issue: **Leverage / Neutral / Overhead.** "What are the highest leverage things that you should exert your influence on — and be okay with certain things working the way they are." Only Leverage issues justify capital. "Escalation also requires energy — you can't do escalations for everything." Be "very, very selective."

Done when the issue has one of the three LNO labels, not left ambiguous.

## Step 3 — Capital budget

- What does this fight cost? (Relationships strained, exec attention drawn, the favor you burn, the precedent you set.)
- Can you afford to *lose* it? Never start a fight whose loss you cannot absorb.
- Is there a zero-capital alternative — an undeniable demonstration instead of an argument (Law 9)? If a prototype, A/B test, or data pull can settle it, route to `manage-the-upward-channel` and make the result loud.

Done when the cost, the affordability of losing, and the existence (or absence) of a zero-capital alternative are all stated.

## Step 4 — GO/NO-GO

**NO-GO** if: Neutral/Overhead issue; truth-seeker motive unresolved; loss unaffordable; demonstration available. NO-GO output includes the let-it-go line: "it could be better, but be okay with it" — plus any demonstration play.

**GO** only if: Leverage issue, affordable downside, influence reframe done, and no cheaper path.

Done when a verdict is reached with its reasoning traced back to Steps 1–3, not asserted.

## Step 5 (GO) — Design the move

**Choose posture — fox or lion (Machiavelli):** "Be a fox to discover the snares and a lion to terrify the wolves. Those who rely simply on the lion do not understand what they are about."
- **Fox:** indirect moves first. Control the options (Law 31): present 2–3 predefined choices that all lead to your outcome. Find the thumbscrew (Law 33): their hidden motivator — craving public praise, fear of looking outdated — and align it with your project. Keep hands clean (Law 26): let a compliance mandate or exec sponsor announce the unpopular part. Outwardly remain cooperative and reliable throughout.
- **Lion:** open contest. Only when the fox route is exhausted or the threat demands it.

**If the move is an escalation strike (removing a toxic dependency, dismantling a rival's hostile project, moving against an exec):** consolidate. "Injuries ought to be done all at one time, so that, being tasted less, they offend less." People "can avenge themselves of lighter injuries; of more serious ones they cannot." One complete, fully prepared strike — evidence assembled, allies pre-aligned, decision-maker briefed — never a drip of partial reprimands that leaves a wounded opponent "plotting revenge."

**Special case — moving against an exec:** never tell the CEO "you need to fire this person" (it indicts the CEO's own judgment). The long game is competence + visibility + letting the exec's failures surface undeniably.

Done when a posture is chosen and, for an escalation strike, the single consolidated move is fully prepared rather than staged as partial reprimands.

## Overuse warnings (read before every GO)

- The strike is a **rare** instrument. Used for minor inconveniences, "your severity will multiply with time" and you become a pariah — severities "badly employed... multiply with time rather than decrease."
- Caught being too cunning (overplayed fox) and you lose the trust that cross-functional leadership runs on.
- Win rate is not the metric. Capital efficiency is: the best operators fight roughly one battle a quarter and win it completely.

## Output format

```
VERDICT: GO / NO-GO
REASONING: [truth-seeker check result, LNO class, capital budget]
IF NO-GO: [the let-it-go framing + demonstration alternative if any]
IF GO: [posture, move design, timing, allies, the one consolidated strike if applicable]
OVERUSE FLAG: [battles fought this quarter; warn if >1]
```
