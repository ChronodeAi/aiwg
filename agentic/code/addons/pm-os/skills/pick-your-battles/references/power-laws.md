# Power Laws — Workplace Translations

Source: The 48 Laws of Power (Robert Greene) and The Prince (Machiavelli), via NotebookLM `a68f8f9e-7e44-490e-b0eb-7313e58e3973`. Each law carries Greene's own reversal — the overuse/misuse condition. Treat reversals as load-bearing, not footnotes.

## The 12 workplace-applicable laws

| Law | Trigger | The move | Reversal (overuse risk) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Never outshine the master** | Presenting to a VP/Director with a fragile ego | Attribute your best ideas to their prior guidance; make them look brilliant so they champion your work | If the master is a falling star about to be fired, don't fear outshining — quietly distance and let them fall |
| **4. Always say less than necessary** | Tense escalation meeting; scope negotiation | State your core requirement and stop talking; silence forces others to fill the void, revealing constraints or conceding | In trust-building contexts (kickoffs), silence arouses suspicion and breeds insecurity |
| **7. Get others to do the work, take the credit** | Massive cross-functional launch without direct authority | Delegate heavily; position yourself to leadership as the orchestrator who synthesized the parts | If too junior, taking all credit makes you look like a parasite — share credit until your status is unassailable |
| **9. Win through actions, never argument** | A stakeholder repeatedly disagrees with your prioritization | Stop debating: run the A/B test, pull the data, build the prototype — prove it undeniably | Passionate argument is occasionally useful as a smoke screen to cover a failure |
| **11. Keep people dependent on you** | Reorg imminent; domain security needed | Become sole possessor of a complex system, critical data, or an exclusive exec relationship | Total dependence breeds resentment; a trapped boss secretly looks for a way to eliminate you |
| **15. Crush your enemy totally** | A toxic colleague actively sabotaging your project/mandate | Don't reprimand or win one meeting — remove their access entirely or escalate until permanently sidelined | Pariah risk. And a merely wounded enemy spends all their time plotting revenge |
| **16. Use absence to increase respect** | Invited to every minor sync; strategic influence waning | Decline lower-level meetings; scarcity raises the value of your voice at major reviews | If new, absence means being forgotten — establish presence heavily first, then withdraw |
| **26. Keep your hands clean** | Enforcing an unpopular decision (deprecating a beloved feature) | Find a cat's-paw: cite a security mandate, compliance law, or have an exec sponsor announce it | Sometimes you must visibly make the hard call yourself to establish decisive authority |
| **31. Control the options** | A powerful but incompetent stakeholder wants to dictate your roadmap | Present 2–3 predefined options that all lead to your outcome; they feel powerful choosing, you set the boundaries | Too-obvious restriction feels manipulative — they rebel and reject all choices |
| **33. Discover each man's thumbscrew** | Need buy-in from a stubborn cross-functional leader | Find the hidden insecurity or core motivation (craves public praise, fears looking outdated) and quietly align it with your project | Push too hard or expose it publicly and you provoke a vicious, irrational backlash |
| **36. Disdain what you cannot have** | A peer baits you into a public turf war | Pay no attention; treating it as trivial preserves status — acknowledging lowers you to their level | Ignoring a genuine, rapidly growing threat makes you look oblivious, not superior |
| **46. Never appear too perfect** | After a massive visible launch, peers grow quiet/envious | Admit a minor harmless process flaw; ask advice on something trivial — be human | Admitting fundamental incompetence or real strategic error ruins you — keep manufactured flaws minor |

## Machiavelli — the strike and the posture

**The Rule of Injury (consolidated strike):** "Injuries ought to be done all at one time, so that, being tasted less, they offend less; benefits ought to be given little by little, so that the flavour of them may last longer." And: "Men ought either to be well treated or crushed, because they can avenge themselves of lighter injuries; of more serious ones they cannot." He who strikes timidly "is always compelled to keep the knife in his hand."

**Properly vs badly used severity:** properly used severities are "applied at one blow and are necessary to one's security, and are not persisted in afterwards." Badly employed ones "multiply with time rather than decrease" — the operator becomes universally hated.

**Fox and lion:** "It is necessary to be a fox to discover the snares and a lion to terrify the wolves. Those who rely simply on the lion do not understand what they are about." Outward posture: always appear cooperative, faithful, reliable — while staying ready to shift the moment the environment turns hostile.

**Judge by results:** "In the actions of all men, and especially of princes... one judges by the result. Let a prince have the credit of conquering and holding his state — the means will always be considered honest, and he will be praised by everybody."

**The great enterprise:** "Nothing makes a prince so much esteemed as great enterprises and setting a fine example" (Ferdinand of Aragon: one large undeniable win after another, each arising from the last, so opponents "have never been given time to work steadily against him").
