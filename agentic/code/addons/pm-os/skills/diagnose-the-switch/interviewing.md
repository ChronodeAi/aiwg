# Switch interview protocol

Use when the artifact is too thin to score the four forces, or when the user is about to talk to a real buyer.

A switch interview is closer to a criminal interrogation than a market-research survey. You don't bring a question list — you bring a method. You start at the moment of purchase and walk the buyer **backward** through the dominoes.

## The documentary setup

Open with: *"I want to make a documentary about how you decided to buy [X]. There's no right or wrong answer. I just want the story."*

This frames you as a curious outsider, not a salesperson. It also gives you permission to dig into mundane detail without seeming weird.

## Walking the timeline backward

Anchor at the moment of purchase. Ask:

- *"Where were you when you signed/clicked buy/handed over the card?"*
- *"What were you wearing? What was the weather like? Who was with you?"*

The mundane physical details jog episodic memory. Once you have the purchase moment in 3D, walk the buyer back domino by domino:

- *"Before that, what happened? Then before that?"*
- *"What made today the day? Why not last week? Why not next week?"* — this finds the **time wall**.
- *"When did you first start thinking about this? What was happening that day?"* — this finds the **first thought**.

## Listening posture

Two markers you cannot let pass:

**Energy spikes.** Sighs, pauses, sudden inflection up or down, voice catching, the word *"finally"*. When energy moves, dig: *"Wait — tell me more about that. Why is that important?"* Energy reveals the emotional push beneath the functional cover story.

**Vague words.** Any time the buyer uses *easy, fast, healthy, convenient, smooth, trust, simple, intuitive,* stop. Run the unpack protocol below. Never accept a vague word at face value — "buyers are liars" not because they mean to be, but because they don't consciously know their own reference points.

## Vague-word unpack protocol

When a buyer says *"I wanted something easy"*:

1. *"Easy like what?"* — force a reference example.
2. *"Easy like a car or easy like a microwave?"* — bracket against two options.
3. *"And what's *not* easy? Give me an example."* — get the opposite reference.
4. *"So if it were like [their not-easy example], you would have walked away?"* — confirm the boundary.

You're done when you can rewrite their vague word as an action: *easy = familiar and repeatable; not easy = inconsistent, depends on conditions I don't control.*

## Minutia question bank

When the buyer goes vague or forgets, fire these. They jog memory more than direct questions:

- *"What time of day was it? Morning or evening?"*
- *"Were you on your phone or laptop? What browser?"*
- *"Did you talk to anyone before you bought? What did they say?"*
- *"What did you look at right before deciding? What did you look at right after?"*
- *"Did anything almost stop you? What was the closest you came to walking away?"*

## Three motivations to listen for

Every buyer has three layers of "fuel." A good interview surfaces all three:

- **Functional:** time, effort, speed, mechanics. *"I love getting a 20-minute workout between meetings."*
- **Emotional:** internal feeling, relief, anxiety reduction. *"I wanted to find something I could get addicted to again."*
- **Social:** how they want to be seen. *"I'd seen friends on Insta with their Pelotons."*

If you only have one of the three at the end of the interview, you're not done.

## Output

A switch interview ends with: the timeline of dominoes named, the time wall identified, the vague words unpacked into action-level reference points, and at least one quote per force ready for [forces.md](forces.md) scoring.
