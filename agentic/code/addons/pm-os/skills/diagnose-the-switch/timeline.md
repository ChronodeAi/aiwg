# Timeline

The buyer moves through six stages. Each transition is caused by a specific event — a **domino** falling. Place the buyer by finding the most recent domino, not by asking how they "feel."

## Stages and markers

**first-thought.** The buyer realizes they have a problem. Space opens in their head for a solution but they are not yet looking. Marker: language like *"I started noticing…"*, *"It hit me that…"*, an inciting event mentioned in passing.

**passive looking.** Aware of the problem, learning, not spending time or money. Marker: reads articles, watches videos, asks friends in passing, but no calendar time blocked, no spreadsheet, no shortlist. Will sit here indefinitely without a time wall.

**active looking.** Spending time and effort to see possibilities. Marker: comparison spreadsheets, demo calls booked, vendors contacted, shortlist exists. Look for the **time wall** — the event that forced active looking. Without a time wall, "active looking" is actually passive looking with extra steps.

**deciding.** Buyer connects the dots, locks in trade-offs, pours expectations in concrete. Marker: comparing 2–3 finalists, raising specific objections, asking about contract terms, asking about onboarding logistics. Anxiety peaks here.

**onboarding.** The **big hire** — initial use. Marker: implementation in progress, first-use stories, "does it work like I expected" tension. Expectations set in `deciding` are tested.

**ongoing use.** The **little hires** — daily use. Marker: habit formed (or not). The product becomes invisible (good) or gets bypassed (bad).

## Dominos

Each stage transition is caused by a domino — a specific event you can name. When placing a buyer, the diagnostic question is not "what stage are they in" but "what was the last domino, and what's the next one." Examples:

- *first-thought → passive:* "my back started hurting again" / "the audit flagged us" / "my daughter went into hospital."
- *passive → active:* "the laptop wouldn't boot" / "the contract renewal landed" / "we missed Q3 numbers."
- *active → deciding:* "the founder of the other vendor ghosted me" / "the holiday is in two weeks" / "the lease expires."
- *deciding → onboarding:* signature, payment, kickoff.

If you cannot name the buyer's last domino, you don't actually know their stage — keep interviewing.

## Why-the-stage-matters quick reference

| Stage | Move that fits | Move that backfires |
|---|---|---|
| first-thought | Story or question that names the struggle | Feature pitch |
| passive | Same as first-thought, plus naming the cost of doing nothing | Asking for a meeting |
| active | Three-option contrast, unpack the value code | One canned pitch |
| deciding | Reduce anxiety, play it out, set expectations honestly | Over-promise |
| onboarding | Concierge the first-use experience | Disappear |
| ongoing | Watch the little hires; reinforce the habit | Up-sell |
