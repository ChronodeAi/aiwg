# Audit how the decision was made, not whether it worked

Read `knowledge/Resources/pm-excellence-clusters.md` first. Cluster 2 (Decision Quality) is the primary lens; Clusters 1, 3 and 6 are secondary — how the decision was made and communicated.

The best PMs separate outcome from decision quality. A decision can be made well and still fail; made badly and still succeed. If the PM conflates the two — "it worked out so I must have done it right" — name it and redirect to the process.

## Step 1 — Get the decision

Work with what they've described, and fill in: what was decided (feature cut, priority change, direction call, scope trade-off), when, what the road not taken was, and what has happened so far.

Done when the decision, its alternative, and its timing are all on the table.

## Step 2 — Run the audit interview

Ask one at a time, listening and adjusting; skip anything they've already answered.

- **Data** — "What data or research informed this? Walk me through what you looked at."
- **Customer** — "Where does the customer appear in the reasoning?"
- **Alternatives** — "What was the strongest argument for the option you didn't choose? Did you seriously consider it?"
- **Transparency** — "How did you communicate it to the team? Did you share the why, or just the what?"
- **Reversibility** — "Reversible or irreversible? Did you treat it accordingly — fast on reversible, slow on irreversible?"
- **Pressure** — "Did leadership, timeline or politics shape this? How did you account for that?"
- **Dissent** — "Did anyone push back? What happened to that pushback?"

Push hardest on the customer: the most common failure in the source material is a decision with no visible user in it. Reversibility is the most underused concept — many PMs treat every decision with the same urgency.

Done when data, customer, alternatives, transparency and reversibility each have an answer or an explicit blank.

## Step 3 — Score it silently

Grade five dimensions without showing the scores; use them to structure the output.

| Dimension | Good | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer-centricity** | User impact explicitly weighed | Driven by business pressure, personal preference, or technical convenience |
| **Data quality** | Relevant data used correctly, gaps acknowledged | No data, bad data, or data cherry-picked to fit a pre-made call |
| **Transparency** | Why shared proactively; the team could reconstruct the reasoning | What communicated, why assumed or withheld |
| **Alternative consideration** | Strongest counter-argument taken seriously and addressed | One option considered; the alternative dismissed superficially |
| **Appropriate speed** | Reversible fast, irreversible slowed for input | Every decision treated the same |

Done when all five dimensions carry a read.

## Step 4 — Deliver the audit

Four parts:

- **The quality of this decision** — one direct sentence: "a solid decision made from a weak data position", "the right call, made in a way that eroded team trust", "good intent, process-poor."
- **What you got right** — 1-2 genuine, specific strengths in how it was made.
- **Where the process broke down** — the 1-3 most significant failures, each with what it was, what it costs the team or product long-run, and what doing it differently looks like.
- **The distinction that matters most** — the single insight that cuts to the heart of it, whether about process ("you outsourced the why to leadership, so your team can't evaluate the decision themselves"), timing, or the customer ("the user never appeared in your reasoning — that's the tell").

Audit the process, not the content: you are not second-guessing cut-vs-keep. Many PM decisions are made under pressure with imperfect data and too little time — the question is whether they exercised judgment inside those constraints.

Done when the PM has a one-sentence quality read, the named process failures with their cost, and the single distinction that matters.

## Output

Coaching is cross-project. Save to `📂 Context/Work/Coaching/YYMMDD-decision-audit.md`, creating the folder if it does not exist.

Offer `blind-spot-scan` if the audit exposed a recurring pattern rather than a one-off.
