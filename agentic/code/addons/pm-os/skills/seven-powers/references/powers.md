# The 7 Powers — tests, false positives, and acquisition plays

Hamilton Helmer's taxonomy. Each power below carries the falsifiable tests that confirm it, the false positives most often mistaken for it, the plays that acquire it, the KPIs that track it, and its characteristic risk. Bracketed values are thresholds to fill from the business context.

## 1. Scale Economies

Per-unit cost drops as volume rises.

**Tests:** fixed-cost share ≥ [X]% and growing, with the unit-cost curve declining against volume; the leader can profitably price [Y]% below a follower without margin collapse; supplier terms improve stepwise at volume thresholds.

**False positives:** purchasing power without declining *unit* costs; temporary discounts.

**Acquire:** concentrate volume (narrow SKUs or segments) to cross step-change thresholds; convert variable cost to fixed (automation, owned infrastructure) only where utilisation ≥ [Z]%; densify demand geographically to lower last-mile and SG&A per unit.

**KPIs:** unit cost vs volume, contribution margin vs share, utilisation %.

**Risk:** diseconomies from complexity; capex lock-in — add stage gates.

## 2. Network Effects

Value rises with each new node.

**Tests:** utility for the average user rises with network size or density (show the curve); churn falls as local cluster density rises; for marketplaces, cross-side elasticity runs both ways.

**False positives:** virality or growth loops with no in-product interaction; data *scale* with no performance lift.

**Acquire:** seed an atomic network — one tight niche at 30–50% density; ensure single-player utility first, then add invitations, matching, and governance; subsidise the harder side and reduce multi-tenant friction (interop, import).

**KPIs:** cluster density, match rate, time-to-first-value, same-side and cross-side retention.

**Risk:** cold start and spam — design governance early.

## 3. Counter-Positioning

A business model incumbents will not copy.

**Tests:** copying would cannibalise the incumbent's profit pool, brand, or operations; your model is viable *now* on different economics; there is observable incumbent hesitation or PR defensiveness.

**False positives:** merely being cheaper; features an incumbent could add painlessly.

**Acquire:** map the incumbent P&L to locate the sacred cows; publish credible commitments (price caps, memberships); design an organisation or process they cannot mirror without upheaval.

**KPIs:** share gains where incumbent exposure is highest, gross-margin durability.

**Risk:** retaliation — prepare wedge markets and switching grants.

## 4. Switching Costs

Economic, operational, or psychological lock-in.

**Tests:** quantified migration cost (time, money, risk) exceeds [N] months of expected benefit; embedded data, workflows, and integrations are not trivially portable; habit frequency ≥ [F]/week and trained users ≥ [T] hours.

**False positives:** stickiness that is really apathy; long terms bought with discounts.

**Acquire:** deepen workflow integration; own the critical data schemas and APIs; build histories and automations; add modules that expand organisational surface area (reporting, compliance, training). Keep export possible but costly to *apply*.

**KPIs:** logo and seat net retention, attach rate of integrated modules, migration attempts.

**Risk:** regulatory pushback — ensure portability and consent.

## 5. Branding

A price premium for identical function.

**Tests:** price premium persists at equal specs, and the willingness-to-pay delta survives post-trial; choice persists under blind-usage-to-brand-reveal tests; meaning is consistent across touchpoints (persona, promise, proof).

**False positives:** performance-marketing lift, temporary hype, celebrity association alone.

**Acquire:** define a category point of view and symbol set; design three moments of truth — purchase, first use, failure; operate a consistency system across narrative, design, experience, and proof assets.

**KPIs:** willingness-to-pay uplift %, aided-to-unaided awareness, repeat purchase at premium.

**Risk:** overextension — protect the promise with operational SLAs.

## 6. Cornered Resource

Exclusive access on attractive terms.

**Tests:** a scarce asset (IP, data, rights, talent, real estate) materially improves value; access is exclusive by law, contract, preference, or scarcity for ≥ [term].

**False positives:** non-exclusive vendors; poachable talent without cohesion.

**Acquire:** secure patents, rights, and exclusive supply; build data compacts and proprietary datasets with consented feedback loops; assemble an A+ team with cohesion and vesting.

**KPIs:** share of revenue tied to the exclusive asset, renewal term, performance delta vs the non-exclusive alternative.

**Risk:** key-person dependency — build redundancy and retention design.

## 7. Process Power

Operational excellence others can observe but not replicate.

**Tests:** simultaneous superiority — cost down *and* quality up — sustained over time; competitors can watch and still fail to copy, because the advantage is cultural and systemic.

**False positives:** SOPs or tooling alone; heroics by a few individuals.

**Acquire:** install a management system (cadence, metrics, kaizen, stop-the-line); build capability ladders, teach-to-learn loops, and supplier development.

**KPIs:** defects, cycle time, unit cost, on-time delivery — all trending better *together*.

**Risk:** fragility to turnover — institutionalise via training and audits.

## Guardrails

- Brand ≠ ad spend. Network ≠ virality. Scale ≠ buying power. Process ≠ checklists. Switching cost ≠ contract penalties alone.
- Two-sided markets: evaluate each side separately; seed one atomic market at a time.
- Regulated or capex-heavy businesses: stress-test capital cycles and permitting.
- Early stage: prefer Counter-Positioning or Cornered Resource; do not force Brand or Process prematurely.

## Scoring rubric

Apply to each power: benefit clarity (0–3), barrier strength (0–3), evidence and metrics quality (0–3), phase appropriateness (0–3), durability in years protected (0–3). Total /15, mapped to confidence 0–100.
