# Kernels and surfaces

Vocabulary for decomposing a workflow step before any automation is proposed.

## Action kernels — what the human is doing

- **Find** — locate the relevant record, message, document, person, or prior example.
- **Extract** — pull structured facts from messy input.
- **Compare** — reconcile two representations of the same truth.
- **Classify** — assign type, status, owner, priority, risk, or account code.
- **Decide** — proceed, hold, escalate, approve, reject, or defer.
- **Compose** — write language: an email, memo, note, checklist, or explanation.
- **Chase** — request missing input, payment, evidence, approval, or clarification.
- **Monitor** — track time, deadline risk, stale work, status, or drift.
- **Approve** — take responsibility for a proposed action or outcome.
- **Repair** — resolve an exception, conflict, relationship issue, or incorrect state.

## Friction kernels — which part of the action hurts

- **Search friction** — finding the right thing.
- **Context assembly friction** — gathering enough surrounding facts.
- **Mapping friction** — connecting one representation to another.
- **Uncertainty friction** — knowing whether the answer is safe enough.
- **Evidence friction** — knowing whether proof is sufficient.
- **Blank-page friction** — deciding what to write or say.
- **Social friction** — saying it with the right tone or relationship posture.
- **Memory friction** — remembering to act at the right time.
- **Attention friction** — deciding what deserves interruption.
- **Legitimacy friction** — making the decision acceptable to others.
- **Accountability friction** — being able to explain who decided and why.

## Surface types — ambient or control

- **Ambient absorption** — system executes quietly; the user sees a summary or audit trail only.
- **Ambient with receipt** — executes quietly, leaves a visible record.
- **Batch control** — many low-risk actions grouped for one quick approval.
- **Exception control** — only uncertain, blocked, or high-consequence items surface.
- **Human-led control** — the human decides, approves, repairs, or takes responsibility.
- **Do not automate** — judgment, relationship, legitimacy, or accountability is too central.

### A step can go ambient when

The correct answer is objective, confidence is high, errors are cheap or reversible, the action is routine, relationship stakes are low, an audit trail is sufficient, or the user has already approved a reusable rule.

### A step needs a control surface when

Money moves, legal or compliance exposure exists, customer or staff relationships matter, evidence is incomplete, confidence is low, precedent is weak, someone must explain the decision later, or the action changes authority, status, or commitments.
