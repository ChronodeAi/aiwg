# Amazon PRFAQ

Author: Amazon (documented by Colin Bryar & Bill Carr)
Source: https://workingbackwards.com/resources/working-backwards-pr-faq/
Book: Working Backwards (St. Martin's Press, 2021)
Use When: New Product, Exec Buy-In, Planning
Don't Use When: Quick Alignment, Build, Feature Work
Best For: Exec Approval, Stakeholder Alignment
Audience: Leadership
Detail Level: Heavy
Time to Complete: Multi-day (iterated across reviews)
Artefact: Document
Advantages: Customer-obsessed, narrative forces clarity, surfaces hard questions early, battle-tested at Amazon since 2004
Limitations: Heavy process, not for small features, requires iterative review culture, overkill for incremental work

---

*"Writing a press release is a forcing function to ensure that the creator of the new product idea is focused on the customer." — Working Backwards*

*Write the press release as if the product has already shipped. Then work backwards through FAQs to figure out how to make it real. The best PR/FAQs go through many drafts with stakeholder feedback.*

---

# PRESS RELEASE

## [Product Name]

*[One-line description that a customer would understand]*

---

**[City, Date]** — [Company name] today announced [product name], a [brief description of what it is] that [key customer benefit].

[Problem paragraph — describe the problem from the customer's point of view. What do customers struggle with today? Why is this problem worth solving? Use specific language a customer would use, not corporate jargon.]

[Solution paragraph — describe how the product solves the problem. Focus on the experience, not the technology. What can the customer now do that they couldn't before?]

[Customer quote paragraph — a fictional quote from a target customer describing why they're excited about this product and what it enables them to do. Make it feel real and specific.]

[How it works paragraph — briefly describe the key mechanics. How does a customer get started? What does the core experience look like? Keep it at the level a press article would describe.]

[Internal quote paragraph — a fictional quote from a company leader explaining why the company built this and what it means for the company's mission.]

**[Product name] is available [availability details — when, where, pricing, how to get started].**

# EXTERNAL FAQ

*Questions the press and customers will ask. Write answers in customer-friendly language.*

**Q: What is [product name]?**
A: [Clear, jargon-free description]

**Q: Who is this for?**
A: [Target customer segment]

**Q: How much does it cost?**
A: [Pricing model]

**Q: How does it work?**
A: [Customer-facing explanation of key mechanics]

**Q: How do I get started?**
A: [Onboarding/availability details]

**Q: How is this different from [existing alternative]?**
A: [Differentiation in terms of customer value, not features]

**Q: What if I need help?**
A: [Support model]

---

# INTERNAL FAQ

*Questions leadership and stakeholders will ask. Be candid about challenges.*

**Q: What problem does this solve for customers?**
A: [Deep explanation with evidence]

**Q: How big is the opportunity?**
A: [TAM, addressable market, revenue potential]

**Q: What products or solutions do target customers use today?**
A: [Competitive landscape]

**Q: How does this create value for customers compared to alternatives?**
A: [Specific advantages]

**Q: What are the challenging problems we need to solve?**
A: [Technical, legal, operational, business model challenges — be honest]

**Q: What new capabilities do we need to build?**
A: [Engineering, infrastructure, partnerships required]

**Q: What are the dependencies?**
A: [Third-party relationships, technology dependencies, regulatory requirements]

**Q: What does the financial model look like?**
A: [Revenue projections, cost structure, path to profitability]

**Q: What is the go-to-market plan?**
A: [Distribution, marketing, launch strategy]

**Q: What are the key risks?**
A: [Top 3-5 risks with proposed mitigations]
