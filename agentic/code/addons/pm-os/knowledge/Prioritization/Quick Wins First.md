# Quick Wins First

Use When: Analysis Paralysis, New to Team, Need to Build Trust, In Delivery, Mid/Low Influence
Author: General (widely attributed)
Don't Use When: Vision Setting/Long-Term Roadmapping, Established Product in Growth Mode, When Foundational Work is Overdue
Best for Prioritising...: Features, Tasks
Artefact: 2x2 (Impact vs Effort)
Advantages: Gets Team Shipping, Builds Credibility, Visible Progress Fast
Limitations: Incremental by Design, Won't Unlock Major New Value, Can Become a Habit That Avoids Hard Work

Read Case Study: https://jexo.io/blog/the-quick-wins-accelerator-that-works/

## Description

Quick Wins First is the strategy of deliberately identifying and shipping high-impact, low-effort items before tackling harder work. The goal is to generate momentum, build team confidence, demonstrate value to stakeholders, and unblock more complex work downstream.

**The process:**
1. Map all candidate items on a 2x2: Impact (high/low) vs Effort (low/high)
2. Identify everything in the high-impact, low-effort quadrant
3. Sequence that quadrant first, before committing to complex work

**When it's genuinely useful:**
- You're new to a team and need to build trust before pushing for larger bets
- The team is stuck in planning loops and not shipping anything
- You need stakeholder confidence before requesting resources for harder work

**The trap to avoid**: Quick wins can become a substitute for strategy. If the high-impact/low-effort quadrant is always empty after the first quarter, and you're still doing only quick wins, the strategy has become avoidance. The point is to *use* early wins to earn permission for harder, higher-value work — not to permanently stay in the easy quadrant.

Most effective as a bootstrapping strategy, not a permanent prioritization philosophy.
