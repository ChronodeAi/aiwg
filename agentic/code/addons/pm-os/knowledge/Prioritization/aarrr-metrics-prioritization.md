# Description

The AARRR metrics prioritization is a framework for measuring and improving the performance of a product or service. It was developed by Dave McClure, an entrepreneur and investor who founded 500 Startups. The framework consists of five stages that describe how users interact with a product or service: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue and Referral.

Acquisition means attracting new users to your product or service through various channels such as ads, social media, blogs, etc. You can measure acquisition by tracking metrics such as number of visitors, sources of traffic, cost per acquisition, etc.

Activation means getting users to have a positive first experience with your product or service and to achieve their initial goals. You can measure activation by tracking metrics such as sign-ups, downloads, registrations, etc.

Retention means keeping users engaged with your product or service over time and preventing them from leaving. You can measure retention by tracking metrics such as churn rate, retention rate, frequency of usage, etc.

Revenue means generating income from your users through various monetization strategies such as subscriptions, ads, commissions, etc. You can measure revenue by tracking metrics such as average revenue per user (ARPU), lifetime value (LTV), customer acquisition cost (CAC), etc.

Referral means getting users to recommend your product or service to others and to spread positive word-of-mouth. You can measure referral by tracking metrics such as net promoter score (NPS), referral rate, viral coefficient, etc.

The AARRR framework helps you prioritize your product development and marketing efforts by focusing on the most important metrics at each stage of the user journey. It also helps you identify bottlenecks and opportunities for improvement along the way.
