# Competition As Obstacle To Extreme Success

Use When: Vision Setting/Long-Term Roadmapping, Unclear Competitive Position, Strategy Confusion
Author: Product Managers Substack
Don't Use When: In Delivery, Tactical Prioritization, Execution Sprint
Best for Prioritising...: Themes/Lanes, Initiatives/Epics
Advantages: Sharpens Competitive Clarity, Forces Vision-First Thinking, Cuts Scope Creep
Limitations: Requires Vision Agreement First, Won't Score Features, Abstract Without Follow-Through

Read Case Study: https://productmanagers.substack.com/p/myopia-that-kills-your-company

## Description

A reframe of competitive strategy: instead of asking "who are our competitors?", ask "what would extreme success look like?" Then define everything that stands between you and that extreme success. *That* is your actual competition.

The insight is that most teams define competition too narrowly (direct competitors) or too broadly (everything). This reframe forces the team to be specific: name the exact obstacles to extreme success, and then debate whether to remove them.

**The two-step process:**
1. Define extreme success in concrete terms — not a vision statement, but an actual state of the world where you've won completely.
2. List everything that blocks that state: internal capability gaps, customer behavior patterns, competitor advantages, market conditions.

What you choose to prioritize should directly attack the biggest obstacles on that list. Initiatives that don't address any obstacle on the list are candidates for removal.

Most effective as a strategy-setting tool before roadmap planning, when the team has lost clarity about what they're actually competing to achieve.
