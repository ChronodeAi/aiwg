# Steady the Ship

Use When: New Senior Leader, Organizational Chaos, Too Much Work, All Work Considered Equally Important
Author: Jason Knight
Don't Use When: Clear Priorities Already Exist, Stable Team, In Delivery
Best for Prioritising...: Themes/Lanes, Initiatives/Epics
Advantages: Provides Clarity in Chaos, Enables Minimum Standards Thinking, Prevents Decision Paralysis
Limitations: Temporary Strategy Only, Requires Stakeholder Communication, Won't Optimize for Growth

Read Case Study: https://twitter.com/onejasonknight/status/1612853603861147649

## Description

A crisis stabilization strategy for the specific situation where everything is considered equally important and nothing gets prioritized as a result. This is common when a new senior leader joins a team with accumulated technical debt, competing stakeholder demands, and no functioning prioritization process.

**The three-phase approach:**
1. **Cut non-essential areas**: Explicitly deprioritize work that doesn't meet a minimum threshold of strategic importance. This is a communication exercise as much as a prioritization one — you need to tell stakeholders which work is being paused and why.
2. **Achieve minimum standards in each area**: Rather than optimizing anything, bring all areas up to a floor. Stop the bleeding before improving.
3. **Stabilize the organization**: Re-establish a functioning prioritization process, clear ownership, and working communication rhythms. Then transition to a growth strategy.

**How to exit this strategy**: Talk to customers and internal stakeholders to determine when "enough has been done" in the stabilization phase. Define the stabilization criteria in advance so the team knows when they're done.

**Prevention**: Build a functioning prioritization process before you're in this state. Good research, clear market positioning, and a regular roadmap review prevent this situation from recurring.

Most effective for new Heads of Product or CPOs entering organizations that have been operating without a coherent product strategy.
