# TOWS

Use When: Vision Setting/Long-Term Roadmapping, Have SWOT Analysis, Need Strategic Options
Author: Heinz Weihrich (extension of SWOT)
Don't Use When: In Delivery, Need Quick Decisions, No Prior Context on Strengths and Weaknesses
Best for Prioritising...: Themes/Lanes, Initiatives/Epics
Advantages: Converts Analysis Into Action, Generates Multiple Strategic Directions, Systematic
Limitations: Requires SWOT Input First, Can Produce Too Many Options, Quality Depends on SWOT Accuracy

Read Case Study: https://www.mindtools.com/pages/article/newSTR_89.htm

## Description

TOWS converts a SWOT analysis into concrete strategic directions by cross-referencing all four quadrants. Where SWOT identifies factors, TOWS generates strategies.

**The four TOWS strategies:**

- **SO (Strengths + Opportunities)**: Use your strengths to take advantage of opportunities. These are your most offensive, high-confidence moves.
- **WO (Weaknesses + Opportunities)**: Overcome weaknesses by pursuing opportunities — or avoid opportunities where your weaknesses make you uncompetitive.
- **ST (Strengths + Threats)**: Use your strengths to minimize threats. These are your defensive plays.
- **WT (Weaknesses + Threats)**: Reduce weaknesses and avoid threats simultaneously. These are your survival/exit strategies — avoid situations where you're both weak and threatened.

**How to use it for prioritization**: Generate candidate initiatives for each quadrant. SO initiatives get highest priority (strong position + clear opportunity). WT initiatives are deprioritized or removed. WO and ST initiatives are contextual — prioritize based on urgency of opportunity or threat.

Best used after you've done a serious SWOT with meaningful specificity. Generic SWOTs produce generic TOWS strategies. The quality of the output depends entirely on the quality of the input.
