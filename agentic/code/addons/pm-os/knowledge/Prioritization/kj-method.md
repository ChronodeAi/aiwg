# Description

The KJ-Method is an interesting mix of independent brainstorming, group dynamics, and democracy. It lets a team be creative and critical in a productive way, where strong personalities and politics take a back seat to the independent perspectives and experience of the team.

A product manager can use this method for product prioritization by following these steps:

- Define a clear question or problem statement that relates to the product goals, such as “What are the most important features for our users?” or “What are the biggest pain points for our customers?”
- Invite a diverse group of stakeholders who have different perspectives and insights on the product, such as developers, designers, marketers, salespeople, customers, etc.
- Provide each participant with sticky notes and pens and ask them to write down their ideas on what features or pain points are important for the product. Encourage them to generate as many ideas as possible without filtering or judging them.
- Collect and display all the sticky notes on a wall or board where everyone can see them clearly.
- Ask the participants to silently sort the sticky notes into groups based on their similarity or affinity. Allow them to move, add, remove or merge sticky notes as they wish until they reach a consensus on how to organize them.
- Ask the participants to label each group with a short phrase that captures its theme. For example, “Security”, “Usability”, “Performance”, etc.
- Ask the participants to rank each group according to its importance or relevance for the product goals. They can use dots, numbers, stars or any other method to indicate their preference.
- Review and discuss the results as a group. Identify which groups are high priority and which ones are low priority for the product development. Use this information as a guide for making decisions about what features to include, exclude or prioritize in future iterations.
