# Pivot Triggers

Use When: B2B, Building a New Product, Internal Product, Limited Influence, Prioritise Opportunities, Sequence Work
Author: Tom Kerwin
Don't Use When: Need Quick Decisions, Vision Setting/Long-Term Roadmapping
Best for Prioritising...: Features, Initiatives/Epics, Lanes/Themes
Artefact: Agnostic
Advantages: Lightweight
Limitations: Draw The Rest Of The Owl🦉, Won't Help With Delivery
Read Case Study: https://tomkerwin.substack.com/p/how-to-do-discovery-and-delivery

# Description

Pivot Triggers are a simple intervention that helps product managers avoid sunk cost bias and escape from zombie initiatives. They are essentially pre-defined conditions that trigger a decision to either continue, pivot or stop a product initiative.

A product manager can use Pivot Triggers to prioritise by following these steps:

- Define the problem and the desired outcome for the product initiative
- Identify the riskiest assumptions and hypotheses that need to be validated
- Design experiments to test those hypotheses and measure progress
- Set Pivot Triggers based on expected outcomes, time frames and resources
- Check in regularly and compare actual outcomes with Pivot Triggers
- Decide whether to continue, pivot or stop based on the evidence

Pivot Triggers can help product managers prioritise by focusing on the most important and uncertain aspects of their product initiatives, avoiding wasting time and money on ideas that won’t pay off, and making data-driven decisions based on feedback and learning.
