# Flywheel

Use When: Vision Setting/Long-Term Roadmapping, Strategy Confusion, Established Product, Building a New Product
Author: Jim Collins
Don't Use When: Tactical Prioritization, In Delivery, Need Quick Decisions
Best for Prioritising...: Themes/Lanes, Initiatives/Epics
Advantages: Reveals Strategic Coherence, Exposes Contradictions, Forces Causal Thinking
Limitations: Time Intensive, Requires Leadership Buy-In, Won't Produce a Scored Backlog

Read Case Study: https://www.pdfdrive.com/turning-the-flywheel-a-monograph-to-accompany-good-to-great-e195296473.html

## Description

Jim Collins' Flywheel model holds that great strategies are self-reinforcing — each action builds momentum that makes the next action easier. The challenge is identifying *your* flywheel: the specific sequence of mutually reinforcing actions that creates compounding momentum for your business.

**The process:**
1. Map the current strategy as a sequence of cause-and-effect steps in a loop
2. Identify which steps are weak links — where does the loop break or stall?
3. Identify which steps are contradicted by current priorities or investment levels
4. Prioritize work that strengthens the weakest links in the flywheel

The value is in the exercise: by forcing the team to draw the flywheel, you expose whether the strategy actually computes. Initiatives that don't appear anywhere in the flywheel are suspect. Initiatives that strengthen a weak link get elevated.

**Common applications**:
- "Our flywheel is: more users → more data → better recommendations → more engagement → more users. But we're investing nothing in recommendations. That's a broken link."
- "We have two strategies pulling in different directions — the flywheel reveals they can't both be true."

Most effective when leadership alignment on strategy is poor, or when you feel like your team's priorities are internally incoherent.
