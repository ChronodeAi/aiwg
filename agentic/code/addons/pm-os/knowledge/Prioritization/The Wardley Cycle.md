# The Wardley Cycle

Use When: Vision Setting/Long-Term Roadmapping, Platform Product, Established Product, Leadership Alignment Needed
Author: Simon Wardley
Don't Use When: In Delivery, Building a New Product, Need Quick Decisions, Tactical Prioritization
Best for Prioritising...: Themes/Lanes, Initiatives/Epics
Advantages: Reveals Evolution Opportunities, Forces Long-Horizon Thinking, Surfaces Platform Potential
Limitations: Complex Framework, Requires Expert Facilitation, Time Intensive, Not Actionable Without Follow-Through

Read Case Study: https://medium.com/org-hacking/pioneers-settlers-town-planners-wardley-9dcd3709cde7

## Description

Wardley Mapping is a strategic framework for visualizing the evolution of components in a value chain — from custom-built innovations to commodities. The Wardley Cycle is a specific strategic pattern: expose your commodity capabilities to the wider market, observe what others build on top of them, then absorb the most successful applications back into your product.

**The cycle:**
1. Identify capabilities in your product that are becoming commoditized (you're maintaining them but they're no longer differentiating)
2. Expose those capabilities as APIs or open platform features
3. Observe the ecosystem: what products and features do third parties build on top?
4. Integrate the highest-value applications back into your core product

This is how successful platform companies grow: AWS exposed infrastructure, observed which services were most used, then built managed services. Salesforce exposed its CRM data, observed which integrations were most valuable, then acquired or rebuilt them.

**Application to prioritization**: Map your product components by their evolutionary stage (genesis → custom → product → commodity). Prioritize building platform/API capabilities around components approaching commodity status, and direct feature investment toward components still in genesis/custom stages where you can differentiate.

Most effective for platform and B2B SaaS products with external developers or integrators, and requires Heads of Product or CPO-level sponsorship to execute.
