# Barbell Strategy

Use When: Vision Setting/Long-Term Roadmapping, Established Product, Have Something to Lose
Author: Nassim Nicholas Taleb (adapted for product)
Don't Use When: Building a New Product, In Delivery, No Existing Value to Protect
Best for Prioritising...: Themes/Lanes, Initiatives/Epics
Advantages: Balances Risk and Safety, Prevents Death-by-Incremental, Forces Explicit Risk Allocation
Limitations: Requires Leadership Agreement on Risk Tolerance, Won't Sequence Delivery, Hard to Apply to Individual Features

Read Case Study: https://medium.com/better-advice/use-the-barbell-strategy-for-risk-taking-in-your-life-f07f60d212f6

## Description

The Barbell Strategy avoids the dangerous middle — medium-risk bets that neither protect you nor give you transformative upside. Instead, you deliberately split your portfolio into two extremes:

- **Safe end (80–90% of effort)**: Investments with a low max drawdown. These protect existing value — core reliability, retention work, incremental improvements to what already works. These won't lose you money.
- **Risky end (10–20% of effort)**: High-risk, high-upside bets. These are exploratory, speculative, potentially game-changing. You can afford to lose this investment because the safe end protects you.

The insight: most teams accidentally invest in the middle — medium-risk features that consume significant resources but have limited transformative potential. The Barbell forces you to either defend or swing big.

**Application to roadmap**: Divide your planning into "protect" work and "explore" work. Debate the ratio, not the individual items. Once the ratio is agreed, assign items to buckets. The safe bucket is deprioritized relative to the risky bucket only if core metrics are at risk.
