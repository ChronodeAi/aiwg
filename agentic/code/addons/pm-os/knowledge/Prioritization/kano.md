## Description

The Kano Model is based on the idea that not all features are of equal importance to users, and some may actually decrease satisfaction if they are not implemented correctly. The model categorizes features into three types: must-haves, performance attributes, and delighters. Must-haves are features that are taken for granted, and their absence would lead to user dissatisfaction. Performance attributes are features that directly correlate with user satisfaction, and the more of them that are present, the higher the satisfaction level. Delighters are features that go beyond user expectations and lead to increased user satisfaction. It is important to carefully frame the study and prioritize features correctly in order to get the most out of the Kano Model.

Kano Model is one of those options you’ve probably heard of but never used.

You’ve probably even tried to model your current features on a 2x2 or explain to someone that certain features may fall into certain categories. 

But I can guarantee that were you to *actually* use the framework, you’d get stuck.

This is a fine-grained tool that may or may not work depending on **what you choose to prioritize** and **how you frame the study.**

**So read the Case Study *very carefully* and try to follow the advice *without skipping steps*** (it’s one of the best guides on how to use the model).
