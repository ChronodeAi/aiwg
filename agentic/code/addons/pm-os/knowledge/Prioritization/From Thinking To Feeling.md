# From Thinking To Feeling

Use When: Vision Setting/Long-Term Roadmapping, Competitive Differentiation Unclear, Building a New Product
Author: Product Managers Substack
Don't Use When: In Delivery, Tactical Prioritization, Established Product with Clear Differentiation
Best for Prioritising...: Themes/Lanes, Features
Advantages: Anchors Strategy in Human Desire, Cuts Through Feature Parity Debates, Guides Differentiation
Limitations: Qualitative, Hard to Validate, Requires Customer Empathy Research, Won't Score a Backlog

Read Case Study: https://productmanagers.substack.com/p/myopia-that-kills-your-company

## Description

A vision-level strategy starter that anchors prioritization in the emotional outcome customers desire, rather than the functional features they request.

The prompt: "Start with how you want your customers to *feel* after using your product. Then ask what people really desire in life."

**Classic examples:**
- After driving a Tesla, how do you feel? Like a rockstar. That feeling drives Tesla's design decisions.
- After driving a Honda, how do you feel? Competent, reliable. Different feeling, different feature set.

The framework forces a question most roadmaps skip: before we decide what to build, do we know what emotional outcome we're optimizing for? Feature requests are means; feelings are ends.

**How to apply it**:
1. Identify 1–2 feelings your product should create post-use
2. Ask: which current roadmap items reinforce those feelings? Which undermine them?
3. Use this as a filter, not a ranking system — items that conflict with the target feeling are deprioritized

Most effective in contexts where differentiation is hard to achieve through features alone (commoditized markets), or where the team defaults to building whatever customers ask for without a clear emotional north star.
