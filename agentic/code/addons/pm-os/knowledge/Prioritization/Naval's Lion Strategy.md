# Naval's Lion Strategy

Use When: Vision Setting/Long-Term Roadmapping, Low Urgency, Strong Execution Capability, Stable Position
Author: Naval Ravikant (via Sahil Bloom)
Don't Use When: Crisis Mode, Startup Finding PMF, In Delivery Sprint, Unclear Strategy
Best for Prioritising...: Themes/Lanes, Initiatives/Epics
Advantages: Prevents Overcommitment, Preserves Optionality, Focuses Energy
Limitations: Requires a Moat or Runway to Execute, Abstract Without Translation to Backlog, Not Useful in High-Urgency Contexts

Read Case Study: https://twitter.com/SahilBloom/status/1424728073505845251

## Description

Naval's Lion Strategy draws from the observation that lions conserve energy by default — they don't chase every prey, they wait and then strike decisively when the opportunity-to-effort ratio is clear.

**The core principles:**
- Keep WIP (work-in-progress) low — don't spread across too many initiatives
- Conserve energy and maintain optionality rather than committing to a full roadmap
- Stay open to new signals and incoming information
- Be confident in execution capability so you can move fast when needed
- When an opportunity emerges, act in days not weeks; think hours not days

**What this means for prioritization**: Don't try to fill every slot on the roadmap. Deliberately leave capacity empty to respond to emerging opportunities. Prioritize ruthless focus on a small number of high-certainty bets, then maintain readiness to execute fast.

**The anti-pattern it counters**: Teams that plan 100% of capacity, leaving no room to respond to market signals, competitive moves, or discovered opportunities. The fully-loaded roadmap is a liability when the environment shifts.

Most effective for established products with a defensible position, sufficient funding, and teams that have demonstrated they can execute at speed when required.
