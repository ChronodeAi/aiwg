# The Wedge

Use When: Building a New Product, Scoping Initial Product, Pre-PMF, Consumer or B2B Early Stage
Author: Every / Divinations
Don't Use When: Established Product, Post-PMF Expansion, In Delivery
Best for Prioritising...: Themes/Lanes, Features
Advantages: Focuses Initial Scope, Prevents Overbuilding, Grounded in JTBD Research, Natural Expansion Path
Limitations: Requires Customer Research First, Won't Work Without Jobs-to-be-Done Data, Not Useful for Mature Products

Read Case Study: https://every.to/divinations/product-wedges-a-complete-guide

## Description

The Wedge is a scoping strategy for initial product development. Instead of building a broad product that covers many use cases, the Wedge focuses relentlessly on one specific use case or customer segment where demand is high and existing solutions are poor.

**The underlying logic:**
- Find a niche where customers have an important job to be done that is poorly served
- Build a product that does that one job exceptionally well — better than any alternative
- Use that wedge position as a launchpad to expand into adjacent markets over time

**The process:**
1. Conduct JTBD interviews to understand what customers are trying to accomplish
2. Map jobs on a 2x2: **Importance** (how critical is it?) vs **Satisfaction** (how well are existing solutions serving it?)
3. Target jobs that are high-importance, low-satisfaction — these are the wedge opportunity
4. Build exclusively for the niche segment that experiences this job most acutely

**Expansion logic**: Once you own the wedge niche, you have a customer base, a brand, and operational capabilities that let you expand into adjacent jobs or segments. LinkedIn started with job seekers; Shopify started with small merchants. The initial wedge creates the beachhead.

Most effective for 0-to-1 products where the team is deciding what to build first and risks building too broadly without customer traction.
